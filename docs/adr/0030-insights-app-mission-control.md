# ADR 0030: The Insights app's own look, and what only it can show (Wave 1)

- **Status:** Proposed (2026-11-19). Wave 1 is built and tested in the local preview with sample data, and an independent review's 10 findings are fixed, as are the 3 gaps its re-review found ([Validation](#validation)); the release's final tests see it in a deployed app. The owner chose the palette on 2026-11-20, Microsoft's colors, and on 2026-11-23 earth tones on cream ([the look](#the-look-earth-tones)). The coordinator kept Mission Control's dark default for this release; it and the service names are the owner's to confirm ([below](#open-for-the-owner)).
- **Date:** 2026-11-19
- **Decision source:** User-directed (2026-11-19): "Make sure the Fabric Rayfin app can do things the Database Hub standalone can't and visually different. Brainstorm some cool things to show case the power of Rayfin apps to show a WOW of that with this." The owner chose "Wave 1 now, in this release" (ideas 1 to 6 of the coordinator's brainstorm). The coordinator made the look's calls (ContosoCare's palette, Mission Control dark by default), which the owner can change. On 2026-11-20 the owner chose the palette: a Microsoft palette, pleasing to the eye, that feels elevated and pleasant to look at. On 2026-11-23 the owner asked for earth tones: "For the overall Fabric App, make the color scheme more pleasing to the eye. Instead of white do creams and do more earth tones. There is some stark contrast for both the light and dark themes."
- **Related:** [ADR 0029](0029-insights-app-dba-console.md) (the DBA console; this ADR replaces its Fabric-teal theme), [ADR 0012](0012-validation-model.md) (statuses), [ADR 0026](0026-one-run-all-session.md) (one Run all, which records the session's plan), [ADR 0028](0028-ask-copilot-in-the-insights-app.md) (Ask Copilot), [the app's README](../../fabric/app/README.md)

## Context

- **The app looked like Database Hub:** Fluent 2 cards in Fabric teal. On a shared screen, the two blurred together.
- **Its hero was "Live now" from Database Hub,** which a real deployment can't show when Database Hub refuses the app's token (R924). What only the app knows was two or three clicks deep.
- **What only the app knows:** what the demo injected and when (`Scenario Runs`, the ground truth), every session's telemetry past Database Hub's 7 days, and the Database Agent beat's documented response. Database Hub sees the symptoms; it can't know the cause the demo planted.
- **Where the plan comes from:** `NB_DatabaseHub_Control`'s Run all passes the session's plan to `NB_DatabaseHub_LiveActivity` at the session's end, which records it in `scenario_runs` last: a `session:<id>` row (profile `session`, target `all`) and one `session:<id>:<NN>` row per beat on T1 to T3 (profile `incident:<scenario>`). T4's and T5's beats aren't recorded; their own metrics show them. So the history has a session's plan only after it ends.

## Decision

Six additions, frontend and read-only only: no new Azure resource, permission, role, private route, Rayfin service, or query in the deploy-time check.

### 1. Mission Control, the home page

- **The service map:** ContosoCare, with each target as the service it serves, its health from the history, a pulse, and a count of the session's events; a node opens its target's page. **Technical** shows each target's peak signal; **Business impact** the same in ContosoCare's words.
- **The session:** the latest session's plan, or the one picked, "as recorded at its end, as of" the newest capture. It's never presented as live: **Database Hub, live** opens Database Hub, and while Manage's function sees a session running, the page says so and links Database Hub. A session the address asks for that isn't in the period shows as missing, with **Show all history** and **Show the latest session**, never as another session in its place. The pages carry a session to each other only when the viewer picked one (the session picker, or **Link to this moment**); otherwise each follows the latest, so a later visit never opens on a session nobody chose.
- **The ribbon and the numbers:** every injected fault on its target's lane (injected, signal visible, answered, recovered), T4's and T5's measured episodes dotted, and the big numbers: faults injected, signal visible, median times to signal and recover, the Database Agent loop's before and after, and T5's throttled share.
- **Overview stays** as a page, one below it in the navigation.

### 2. "Did we catch it?": the scoreboard

For each fault in `Scenario Runs`: when its signal was visible in the telemetry, when it was answered, and when it recovered, with the times to signal (injected to visible) and recover (injected to recovered), per engine and per fault type across the newest sessions (3, and the one picked).

- **"Signal visible in telemetry,"** never "detected by Database Hub": the app can't read what Database Hub raised, and Database Hub shows telemetry 3 to 15 minutes after its sample time. The time is the first bin's sample time.
- **It says what it verifies (2026-11-20):** it checks each injected fault for that fault's expected signal, in its own window, so it never reads as blind detection. "Did we catch it?" has the subtitle "Verified against the injected faults: each one's expected signal, in its own window"; the column and the count say **Signal seen**, not "Caught"; and the times are **time to signal**, not "time to detect", in the app and its postmortem. The owner can change the words back.
- **Measured per signal, with the analysis notebook's own rules** (`src/lib/scoreboard.ts`): the families each scenario raises (`SCENARIO_SIGNALS`), the episode levels (`EPISODE_RULES`), the CPU correlation's 1.5 times and 10 points, and its 15 minutes of telemetry lag. A bin shows the signal at its episode level, or at 1.5 times its baseline when that's higher (for CPU, at 1.5 times and 10 points above its baseline when that's lower than 80 %), for the rule's bins in a row. The baseline is the median of the target's bins within 2 hours, outside the windows of faults that raise the same signal; with fewer than 5 such bins, the level alone. Each row says how it was measured.
- **Each fault in its own bins' step:** the history mixes bin sizes (the notebook bins a window of 120 minutes or less by the minute, 720 by 5, and longer by 15: a session of about 110 minutes or more, `stop-demo.ps1 -SaveHistory`, and deploy's snapshot), so a fault's step is the median gap between the target's bins in its window (or the hour around it), and its baseline reads bins of that size only, since a wait sums over its bin.
- **Recovered:** the first of 2 bins in a row under the bound, within 30 minutes of the fault's window. When the next fault with the same signal starts first, or the telemetry ends, the row says so instead of a time. A T5 episode recovers only at the notebook's measured recovery (one whose 429s only stopped has no recovery time), and a T4 episode whose metrics break off ends there, with its recovery unknown, never "still at the capture's end".
- **A query that fails** is named on the page with **Retry**, beside what still shows; when the telemetry fails, nothing is scored, rather than every fault "not visible", and the faults injected still count from the plan.
- **Answered:** the Database Agent beat's response (the run forces the last good plan; its documented times), or a reset. Other faults have no response step, and the scoreboard says so rather than inventing one.
- **T4 and T5:** their beats aren't in `Scenario Runs`, so they show as measured episodes in their own metrics (CPU and read replica lag; normalized RU and 429s), without a time to signal.
- **Not visible:** a fault whose signal didn't show is a **Warning** with why (ADR 0012); one with no telemetry for its window is a **Warning** too, unless the notebook's own check of its incident window passed (then it's visible, without times), and a scenario with no signal in the history is **Not supported**.

### 3. The business impact lens

- **One file maps targets to ContosoCare's services** (`src/lib/business.ts`): T1 Billing, T2 Front desk, T3 Scheduling, T4 Reporting and its read replica the reports' DR copy, T5 Patient events. They come from the demo's story (the baseline's front desk, billing, and scheduling workload; T3's visit lookup; T5's `patient-events`); which of T1 and T2 is billing is this file's choice.
- **Impact sits next to the measurement it comes from:** "Patient events: 35 % of requests throttled for 9 min (normalized RU peak 100 %, 429s on …)". Every number is measured; there's no user latency, revenue, or patient count, because the history doesn't measure them.

### 4. The postmortem

- **Write the postmortem** on a session (Mission Control, the scoreboard, the replay): **Download Markdown** writes a deterministic postmortem from the data alone (summary, timeline, impact, cause, response, times to signal and recover, follow-ups, how it was measured, and the queries behind it), so it works without a GitHub sign-in. **Copy** copies it.
- **Draft with Copilot** asks the viewer's own GitHub Copilot through the existing `useCopilot().ask` contract, with the same rows and queries and a structured template (`src/lib/postmortem.ts`); nothing in `src/copilot/` changes. No OneLake storage in Wave 1.
- **It waits for the telemetry:** while the telemetry hasn't loaded, nothing is measured, so **Write the postmortem** says why and waits for **Retry**. What was injected always comes from the plan: a session's faults are never "nothing injected" for want of their scores.

### 5. The cinematic replay

One scrubber drives the service map (its states and a shockwave on each fault), all five engines' charts, and the event ribbon (injected, signal visible, answered, recovered), with "Next: … in N min" from the plan relative to the playhead. **Link to this moment** copies the frame's address with the session and the minute in it (the app's view-in-address mechanism, ADR 0029). Inside Fabric, that address is the app's hosted URL, which opens only from the app's item (the app's README, known limitation), so the toast says so: the browser reopens the app where it was left. The address takes the moment (the minute; the session only when the viewer picked one) once it holds still for a second, on a pause or a seek, never at each step of the playback or a drag: WebKit refuses more than 100 address changes in 10 seconds. A screen reader hears play, pause, and each seek, never each step.

### 6. The kiosk

A chrome-free view (`#/kiosk`) that rotates Mission Control (20 seconds), the scoreboard (20), and the replay (45, playing the session through), for a booth or NOC screen. Space pauses it (WCAG 2.2.2), the arrows change scenes, and Esc leaves for Mission Control. **Full screen** uses the Fullscreen API when the frame allows it; otherwise the kiosk says to press F11. A screen reader hears the scene and whether it's paused, never the countdown. The kiosk keeps showing its last history while a query refreshes or after one fails (the app's queries keep their last result, `useQuery`'s `stale`), and asks again every 30 seconds; once a retry loads, its queries refresh with the cache's 5 minutes again.

### The look: earth tones

The owner's choice (2026-11-23): creams instead of white, earth tones, and softer contrast in light and dark. Until then, Microsoft's colors (2026-11-20), and before them ContosoCare's navy with cyan and magenta.

- **Warm neutrals for Fluent's grays:** every gray Fluent 2 uses becomes a warm one (`WARM_NEUTRALS` in `src/theme/identity.ts`, `warmTheme` in `fluent.ts`), hover, pressed, selected, and disabled states included, so Fluent's own controls, menus, dialogs, and message bars follow without a change to them. Light: ivory cards (`#FCF7EE`) on a sand-cream page (`#F2EADB`), soft taupe borders, and text in a soft espresso (`#3E322A`), 11.6:1 on a card instead of 15.5:1. Dark: espresso a step lighter than Fluent's near-black (a `#221B17` page and `#322924` cards) and cream text (`#F0E9D9`), 11.7:1 on a card instead of 14.5:1. Shadows in a warm brown, a fifth lighter in light.
- **The brand:** terracotta (`TERRACOTTA_BRAND`, `#99512D` at 80) on buttons, links, the current page, and the Copilot mark (terracotta into plum). `APP_BRAND` is the one swap point; Microsoft blue (`MICROSOFT_BRAND`) and Fabric's teal (`FABRIC_BRAND`) stay for a one-line swap back.
- **Statuses in earth tones, with their icons and words:** brick for Fail, ochre for Warning, pine for Pass, and slate for Manual action required (`EARTH_STATUS`), apart by lightness as well as hue, so they stay apart under protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia; Fluent's message bars and badges take the same families (`EARTH_STATUS_FAMILIES`) instead of Fluent's red, orange, and green.
- **Accents, used sparingly:** slate, moss, ochre, and clay mark T1 to T4 (the map's node, the target's chip, its chart series) and the charts' first four categories; plum and verdigris mark T5 and T4's read replica; stone and rose are the charts' last two (`EARTH_ACCENTS`). Never a large fill.
- **Elevated:** a panel a layer above the stage (the app's neutral backgrounds 3, 2, and 1 in dark; 3, 1, and 4 in light), the theme's shadows (`shadow8` under a stage panel, `shadow4` under the app's cards and `shadow8` on a hovered tile), and one restrained sage wash at the stage's top; no grid, and no layout change. The stage's accent is sage, calm (the healthy pulse, the answered incident, the current item), and its alert brick (`src/theme/stage.ts`), apart from the terracotta on buttons and links.
- **High contrast and Windows forced colors stay as they were:** Fluent's `teamsHighContrastTheme`, the contrast tokens, and the system's selection colors on the current page, scene, and incident (F22).
- **Mission Control, the scoreboard, the replay, and the kiosk sit on a dark stage** until the viewer picks a theme; a picked light or high-contrast theme applies to them too.
- **Visualization first:** a full-bleed map and a time ribbon, not a card grid. **Motion with a purpose:** health pulses and an incident shockwave, both off under `prefers-reduced-motion`, which the replay honors too.
- **Accessibility stays:** the contrast tests hold each pair to WCAG 2.2 AA, 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for marks and controls: the app's status, target, and chart colors, and Fluent's text, brand text, links, strokes, and focus ring, on cards, the navigation, the page, and the stage, and on the fill of a hovered or pressed row, subtle button, button, or menu item; a Fluent Link's pressed or selected color, a transparent button's hovered or pressed text, and a hint on those surfaces at rest, since no Fluent Link sits in a row and hints sit on cards and panels, and a subtle button's hovered or pressed icon at 3:1 on its fill; the plain links in a table or on the Links page, in the theme's link color, at rest and in a hovered or pressed row; the chart's incident band's label with the band over each of those surfaces; and message bars and every badge the app shows, each on its own fill or the surface. High contrast keeps Fluent's own hover and pressed fills, which these tests don't cover. The tests also hold body text between AAA's 7:1 and 12.5:1, keep the statuses, the targets, and the stage's accent and alert apart under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia, find no literal color in the app's stylesheets, and pin the brand to terracotta, the targets to the earth accents, and the stage to the app's warm neutrals and shadows; every map node and control is reachable by keyboard, with a label, and every fault is a row in the scoreboard's table; status always has a shape and a word (ADR 0012).
- **The bundle:** the four pages load lazily, so the main chunk stays about where it was (+3.5 KB raw, about +1 KB gzip, from the routes).

### Open for the owner

1. The palette: **decided by the owner:** Microsoft's colors on 2026-11-20, then earth tones on cream on 2026-11-23, app-wide ([the look](#the-look-earth-tones)).
2. Mission Control dark by default (kept by the coordinator for this release), or following the system (`stageTheme`).
3. The service names, T1 Billing and T2 Front desk especially (`SERVICES`).

## Consequences

- **Easier:** the app opens on what only it knows, in a look that's clearly its own, with Database Hub one click away for the live view. The same numbers serve the map, the scoreboard, the replay, the postmortem, and Copilot.
- **Harder:** the scoreboard mirrors the notebook's rules (`SCENARIO_SIGNALS`, `EPISODE_RULES`), so a change there needs the same change in `scoreboard.ts`; the tests pin both sides' numbers on the app's side only.
- **Accepted:** the plan is never live, only "as of" the session's end. T4's and T5's beats have no time to signal until the notebook records them (Wave 2). The scoreboard reads only the catalog's existing queries and the incident waits' queries, so the deploy-time check (`FABRIC.APP.QUERIES`, 21 queries) is unchanged.

## Validation

- **Built and tested (2026-11-19):** `npm run verify`; unit tests that fail without each piece: the scoreboard's detect, response, and recover times, the business map and its wording, and the postmortem's Markdown (`src/lib/mission.test.ts`); the tokens' contrast and the stage's dark default (`src/lib/app.test.ts`); and the pages, the kiosk's rotation, pause, and Esc, and reduced motion (`src/pages/mission.test.tsx`).
- **Reviewed (2026-11-19):** an independent review of 75fa62d found no way for `s` or `r` to reach DAX or the page unescaped, and 10 issues, each fixed with a test that fails without its fix: a fault read at the smallest bin step in the period (High); replay playback that wrote the address at every step, which WebKit stops after 100 in 10 seconds and which took the app down (High in Safari); failed queries shown as measured results; T5 episodes whose 429s only stopped called recovered; a kiosk that blanked when its cache expired and stayed so after a failed refresh; T4 episodes that broke off called ongoing; the postmortem's "every signal showed and recovered" when some hadn't; the DR copy at **Pass** without data; countdowns announced every second; and an unknown session shown as the latest. With them, high contrast's selected and hovered navigation item (white on cyan, 1.46:1) and two layout fixes on the map.
- **Re-reviewed (2026-11-19):** the re-review of b14180f verified 7 of those fixes and found 3 gaps, each fixed with a test that fails without its fix: a query the kiosk's retry ran never refreshed again (`useQuery` read the cache only until a refetch); with the telemetry failed, the postmortem said nothing was injected and the kiosk counted 0 faults; and the pages' buttons took the latest session along as if picked, so a later visit could open on "isn't in this period".
- **Screenshots** from the sample-data preview (Mission Control in dark, light, and high contrast, the business lens, the postmortem, the scoreboard, the replay mid-scrub, and the kiosk's three scenes), with the release's handoff.
- **The earth tones (2026-11-23):** `npm run verify`; tests in `src/lib/app.test.ts` that fail with the earlier palette: every pair at AA on the warm surfaces at rest, Fluent's message bars and badges included; body text between 7:1 and 12.5:1; no Fluent gray left; the statuses at 0.09 or more apart in OKLab under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia (0.022 to 0.051 before), the targets at 0.07 or more (0.015 to 0.059 before), and the stage's accent and alert at 0.08 or more; the Copilot mark's glyph at 3:1 on its fill; and no literal color in the app's stylesheets. Before-and-after screenshots of Mission Control, Root cause, Estate, and the Copilot dock, in light and dark, from the sample-data preview, with the handoff.
- **Reviewed (2026-11-23):** the independent review of 2b0e46b0 found three contrast failures that it added, each fixed with a test that fails without its fix: in dark, the brand's text and the Fail and Manual statuses on a hovered row or subtle button (4.39:1 to 4.45:1; now the brand's text one ramp step lighter, `#EE8C8B` and `#80ABD8`), and the chart's incident band's label with the band over a card (4.23:1; the band now at 8 % instead of 14 %); and, at Low, Pass, Warning, and captions on a pressed row in light (4.12:1 and more; a pressed fill is now neutral background 4) and a pressed link on a dark card (3.13:1; now the ramp's 110). Testing the hover fills found a fourth: T5's mark in the target filter's menu on a hovered menu item in dark, 2.89:1, so a hovered button or menu item now takes a hovered row's fill. With them, the agent panel's two outline badges, drawn in Fluent's static inverted color for a dark fill and so invisible on a light card since before the earth tones, now take Fluent's informative color, with a test that every badge the app shows is legible. Its re-check found one more in dark: a transparent button's pressed text and a subtle button's pressed icon, still Fluent's (3.13:1 on a card and 2.95:1 on a pressed fill), now the ramp's 110 (5.28:1 and 4.98:1); and, with it, the plain links in tables and on the Links page, in the browser's blue until then, now take the theme's link color.
- **Pending:** the deployed app in the release's final tests, on a real session's history.
